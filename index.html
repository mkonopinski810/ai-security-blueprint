01

Operating philosophy

Clear the many low-risk cases quickly; reserve scrutiny for the few that are material.

Most AI use across the firm is low-risk productivity gain. Value comes from a fast, credible front door, not from being the office of "no." AI risk is a new surface on existing risks, so the program extends current controls rather than duplicating them.

Principle 01

Extend, don't duplicate

Bolt AI-specific criteria onto the existing vendor-risk, GRC, and design-review processes. A lean function multiplies through others' workflows, not a parallel empire.

Principle 02

Visibility before control

You can't govern what you can't see. Inventory of sanctioned and shadow AI comes first; controls follow the map, not the other way around.

Principle 03

Risk-tiered, not uniform

Depth of review scales with data sensitivity, autonomy, and blast radius. Uniform scrutiny is slow scrutiny, and slow gates kill safe adoption.

02

Framework backbone

Anchored to recognized standards, so the program is auditable on day one.

No invented taxonomy. A spine a regulator or external assessor already recognizes.

Operating structure

NIST AI RMF

Govern · Map · Measure · Manage as the functional shape of the program, and the structure that maps cleanly onto each control domain below.

Management system

ISO/IEC 42001

The policy, process, and continuous-improvement scaffolding, and a future certification target that signals maturity to clients and regulators.

Threat library

MITRE ATLAS · OWASP LLM Top 10

The technical catalog feeding threat modeling: adversarial ML tactics and the LLM-specific failure modes that design reviews test against.

Plan of attack

Seventeen milestones across four phases, each with a clear definition of done.

Horizons are indicative; the sequence is deliberate. Every milestone unblocks the next and produces evidence, not activity.

04

Control domains

Four domains carry the mandate, each an extension of an existing capability.

GOV · Governance & Strategy

Own the framework and the front door

The framework, policy, firm-wide inventory, reporting, and regulatory watch: the spine everything else hangs from.

  • Maintain the AI risk framework: model risk, data privacy, adversarial threat, third-party AI, regulatory.
  • Inventory sanctioned and shadow AI with risk profiles.
  • Regular risk reporting on threat trends, vendor posture, and control gaps.
  • Track the regulatory environment and map obligations onto AI data flows.
TPR · Vendor & Third-Party AI

The largest real-world surface

AI arrives inside tools the firm already runs, often via a silent feature update. Monitoring material change is as important as the first review.

  • Evaluate model governance, data handling, output reliability, auditability, incident response.
  • Tiered register of third-party AI integrations.
  • Re-assessment triggered by model swaps, new AI features, ownership change.
  • Engage vendor security and product teams to drive remediation.
THM · Threat Modeling & Architecture

Where the technical depth shows

Model the LLM and agentic attack surface and feed it into existing design and pipeline reviews.

  • Prompt injection (direct and indirect), model inversion, data poisoning, adversarial input.
  • Agentic and MCP-connected risks: excessive agency, over-broad tool scopes, confused-deputy chains.
  • Finance-specific content threats: deepfake fraud, synthetic phishing, automated social engineering.
  • Embed AI controls into deployment pipelines and system design reviews.
INT · Internal Program Oversight

Own the deployments the firm runs

Be the security and risk point of contact for internal AI, starting with the platform already in production.

  • Verify data is not used for training; configure SSO and SCIM through Entra ID.
  • Set retention, access control, and logging aligned to compliance and eDiscovery.
  • Run the use-case approval framework: approve, conditionally approve, or decline.
  • Support audit and compliance reviews with evidence collection.
05

Risk tiering

One rubric decides how hard to look, and how fast a case clears.

Tier on three axes; the highest axis sets the tier.

Data sensitivity
Public → internal → client-confidential → regulated / MNPI.
Autonomy
Assistive output a human reviews → agentic action taken on the firm's behalf.
Integration depth
Standalone tool → connected to systems of record and live data sources.
Tier 1: Elevated

Full review · senior sign-off

Threat model, vendor assessment, and contract terms required. Conditional approval with compensating controls or decline.

Tier 2: Moderate

Standard assessment

Documented use case, data-handling check, and control review against the framework before approval.

Tier 3: Routine

Fast-track · attest & log

Self-attestation against acceptable use, recorded in the inventory. Cleared quickly so the program stays a path, not a wall.

Regulatory watch

EU AI ActAI-specific

The only AI-specific law on this list. Risk-tiered and extraterritorial, so it reaches EU offices and any AI output used in the EU.

DORAThird-party · EU

The EU operational-resilience regime for financial entities. Its ICT third-party risk rules are the legal lens for vendor-embedded AI.

SECDisclosure

As an NYSE-listed registrant, AI-driven cyber incidents can be material and disclosable, tying AI risk to the board.

FINRABroker-dealer

The firm's broker-dealer entity means existing supervision, recordkeeping, and communications rules already extend to AI use.

NY DFSState cyber

Its 23 NYCRR 500 cybersecurity rules and AI guidance reach DFS-regulated entities, reinforcing governance and third-party controls.

SOXReporting controls

AI that touches the financial-reporting chain falls within internal-control-over-financial-reporting scope.

GDPR / CCPAPrivacy

Govern what personal data flows into AI tools and vendors: lawful basis, data residency, and automated decisions.
